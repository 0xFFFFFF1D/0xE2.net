I'll write something kinda sappy for the first "real" blog post on here. 

My girlfriend and I were friends before we started dating---the exact circumstances
of our meeting is a little murky for both of us, but it was probably through Instagram.
She was maybe the first other Asian transsexual I had met in my life, and 
we hit it off in record time. I was, at the time, trapped in a tailspinning relationship
that had been going on for two years too long at the time with a partner that I had
started seeing in high school and somehow dragged the relationship across state lines.
Maybe it was cheating, maybe it wasn't---call it what you want, but know that it was
a relationship that I was desperate to escape and one that I had tried to leave before.

I think I knew I loved her when she texted me telling me that she had forced herself 
to throw up again and asked if she could come over, and I found myself Googling how to 
mix up a baking soda rinse to neutralize the acid on her teeth. I sat with her in
common room of my sophomore year dorm and watched her swirl the off-white around
through her teeth and then get up and walk over to the bathroom underneath the stairs[^1]
to spit it out. She said she loved me and asked if it was okay that she said she loved
me because she didn't want to "lovebomb" me. I said I loved her too.

Like I'm sure many others have done, I came to summary conclusion at the ripe old
age of 12 that I was unlovable and as such had nothing to really offer the world. 
Many, many, many rounds of hospitalizations and psychiatrists and therapists and
medications and non-prescription pills and drinks and 12-step groups and unfulfilling sex
and gorging myself on frankly ridiculous amounts of food and exercise and not-exactly-white
powders and crystals and television shows and movies later, I found myself loved.

Somewhere along the way---probably at the Saturday night meeting of Narcotics Anonymous
at the First Church of the Nazarene in Lynn, to be honest---I realized that the only
thing I ever wanted in life was to love. I `/l[io]ved/g` to love. When I first got clean,
the one of the first things that made itself clear to me was the importance in helping
your fellow addict. I met up again with a woman from my partial program months after
we had both discharged and I picked her up to attend an NA meeting in Southie. 
She told me about how difficult it was tricking out of her halfway house, and I sympathized. I asked her
about how good the heroin was back in the double aughts, and she said that that 
shit was so pure it was almost hallucinogenic. She told me that she was still smoking
meth, and I told her that I couldn't really blame her. Afterwards, she said she had
enjoyed the meeting, and I dropped her off at a convenience store (maybe it was
a packie? I can't really remember) not too far from her halfway house. She said
she was looking to get a place in Cleveland. I haven't heard from her since.

A good friend of mine recently got back from Italy---she'd been studying abroad there
for a semester. Maybe a week or so into the spring semester, we ran into each other
at a common area in the middle of campus, and quickly discovered that we shared
a 5-hour block of free time twice a week together. She invited me and my girlfriend
over for dinner on a Wednesday, and it quickly became a weekly thing. We see each
other just about every day now. 

I can't say we're terribly productive during our shared five hours of free time
together. Most often we shoot the shit for so terribly long we end up not just
conversationally beating dead horses but rather running glue factories and horse
mincemeat slaughterhouses. She was over for an all-nighter of supposed academic
productivity and we spent maybe a combined 3 hours doing work and the rest gabbing
our stupid mouths off about deferred cishomosexual desire and other miscellaneous
bullshit. We kissed, twice, very briefly and talked about the lack of sexual desire.

During our latest blizzard in sunny, sunny metro Boston, I hand-delivered her 
a quart cup of stew, a rosary, and a small container of homemade tiramisu. The other
day, she said she thought the unrequited homoromantic nature of our friendship 
was "tragic"---I countered and said that it didn't feel tragic for me. I had been
thinking about making that tiramisu with the double dark chocolate Milano® cookies
sub ladyfingers for maybe a year at that point---maybe she was just the impetus for 
change.

I learned how to make 麻婆豆腐 because of how much I saw that you liked it 
at that Chinese place in Central. 

I had recipes for Spaghetti all'Assassina saved for years but I wanted to make
something interesting for you, even if it didn't come out great. 

I started smoking L&Ms because you said they smelled nice---I probably would have
just gone back to Luckies cause they're cheaper.

You showed me how to use a *greca* and taught me to love *queso frito*.

I always put extra 麻辣 wherever I can when I'm cooking for you.

I made extra stew because I knew you were sick.

I usually say "oh hello, beast" when passing a dog on the street since you 
say that too.

When I see dice I think of when you would visit me in the psych ward and bring
me food and play Yahtzee with me.

You reminded me of my childhood fascination with religion and spirituality and
reminded me that I wanted to pray the rosary meditatively more than anything.

I take from the bottom row of my Trident gum packs now.

When I made you that salmon teriyaki and I dropped it off at your place in Brighton 
you said the sauce tasted just like how your mom made it for you. 

I wake up next to you and I remember waking up still high and alone and how different
things are now.

Some days when I feel myself melting into the floor beneath me I make myself move
just to cook for you.

My T-R classes might be unbearable if we didn't spend so much time bullshitting every
week.

Who else would have stayed with me as my using got worse?

<div class="asterism"></div>

Much of the time it feels as though the world does not love me like I love the
world. I still love you anyway---what else is there to do?
