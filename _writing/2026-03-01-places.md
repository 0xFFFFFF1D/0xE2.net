You told me that the first time you knew that you loved me was when we were walking
back from some show in Allston and we passed by a Chinese place with a big glowing
sign promising "HOT SOUP". In your memory, I turned to you with my head tucked neatly
into my hood with wide, wide smile and said, over that ridiculously chilly Boston
wind rushing between us, "hot and nutritious soup!" A little inside joke---the first 
words of our two-person language community.

<div class="asterism"></div>

Porter's where you snapped at me, after having spent an unsolicited weekend for my
birthday with me. There was no real way that conversation was going to go down
well. 30 minutes to South Station when your train left in 40 was already pushing it 
but I saw your expression drop when I told you I really didn't appreciate it
when you pressed your lips into mine while I was so drunk I could barely stand just
12 hours earlier and I knew I was going to later have to cough up $60 for a FlixBus
to disentangle you from me because there was just no way we were going to make it 
South Station, no way, I thought, as you ran out the train doors at Porter. We 
argued and argued and argued and after seeing you motion suicidally towards the tracks
I gave it up and said that it was fine, and the future unfolded like I thought it would.

<div class="asterism"></div>

It's hard to really say what the exact chemical cocktail I was overdosing on
last year---Massachusetts General Hospital would have you believe I was just
drunk and an unconfirmed amount of vilazidone, but they don't test for
dextromethorpan in the standard urinalysis. At some point in the night I laid down
on my floor to die and I felt my heart beat faster and faster and my blood vessels
tighten down to wires and I thought about how I would never marry you and how much
I would miss you---wherever I was going---and how I hoped that this wouldn't destroy
you forever and that you'd move on and that love would find you again, that someone
else would pick up the pieces I was leaving behind; my final shirking. 

<div class="asterism"></div>

The dining room was really quite dimly lit (I guess college students hate to 
have the overheads on) and some friend of yours I barely remember was visiting. 
You were still in the throes of homosexual anhedonia---yet to have deracinated 
yourself from this and embraced a heterosexuality---and so I was over. 
We sat at opposite ends of a somewhat peculiarly long wooden table of 
university-provided chic and the friend sat in between us. For reasons lost to 
atrophying neurons, this out-of-town friend kept leaving the room, and every time
you would rush over and kiss me for just long enough so that it felt risky but 
not long enough that they would actually see and then you would rush back to 
your seat and we would pretend like nothing really happened, except for our respective smirks and heartbeats. 
Maybe nothing really did happen.

<div class="asterism"></div>

I must have been eating when you called with your voice shaky and afraid, it was dark
out and you'd gone off walking in a depressive haze, only now mustering the wherewithal
to cry out for help from the rain. With your location my girlfriend and I piled into
the car with abandon and slammed my poor old boxy Volvo into gear, rather ungracefully
puttering all the way over to Winchester where we saw you and your blue raincoat so
thoroughly soaked through it may as well have been wool sitting on a church bench 
staring at the floor. Through a horrible collision of circumstance I had just washed my
blankets the day prior and had found out that the drier in the basement was woefully
inadequate for removing even a percentage of moisture out of my thick comforter, and
I felt terrible swaddling you in something still damp but it was still better inside
and warm and watching Seinfeld than outside in the drizzling night all alone.

<div class="asterism"></div>

In a typically pathetic fashion I had psyched myself out from saying that I loved
you, holding back as I drove you back to your place at another unreasonably late hour.
Even having had promised my friends that I would say something about how I felt I still
pussied out because I thought that it was just untenable---another thing to be disappointed 
by. I watched you walk up your stoop, wave goodbye, and disappear into your flat, 
and I sank into my chair, collecting my thoughts, waiting for my heart to rise back up between
my lungs, I heard you knock---I heard you knock on my window? I rolled it down, 
head cocked: you said you had left your phone in my car, and I pulled out my phone 
and turned out the flashlight and yes, you had, it had fallen into the crevice between the 
passenger seat and the door and landed alongside dog hair from the previous
owner and some pennies. I handed the phone to you and watched you turn around when
I called out to you to wait and you turned back around with a half-mouth opened
smirk. I swallowed what felt like tonsil stones and I said that I thought I loved you,
your face turning to a full smirk, and after a beat that sent my heart back
down towards my gallbladder, you said you thought you loved me too.
