The thought was obvious, maybe just to one of them, that every abandoned building–obviously they’re all abandoned, who could possibly be living all alone in these wooden cages all alone away from everyone–all were torture chambers, someone’s personal hell living out in every single one of them; needles in eyes, vice grips ripping out fingernails, sliced ligaments, blood choked screams—it was all happening to someone in every single pine-clad house and every concrete office building in the dozens of industrial parks that melted together in the view of the windshield, that psychic agony was obvious.

It never occurred to either to turn the heater down.

Neither moved. A light drizzle had started not too long ago, hazing over that harsh fluorescence of civilization with a screen that swirled the world into a deeper dream. A street light some half mile down the road flickered for the last time and died, returning a small slice of this waking nightmare to the arboreal sea. They both looked outside at the soupified world, through misted windows, thoughtscapes unfurling in such disparate ways they might as well have been strangers. The gas station was closed, but the lights were still on, with the doors locked. Neither noticed, and neither moved. 

|Can I tell you something?||
||Sure.|

The streetlights went out in a cascade, like stereotypical lemmings off a cliff, one after another. The shadows cast by the sterile lights seemed to lengthen on command. There was something not-quite-right about the receding light, they were sure of this, it wasn’t the same, ancient, dark that swallowed the lonely evil of those slaughterhouses out in that ocean of pine. No, this was a puerile kind of nothing, some sort of young hatred, not opposing the dungeons of agony that were all around them but instead in cahoots with. 

|I’m scared.||
||Of what?|

There were only the overhead lights of the Sunoco. Something skittered underneath the car, something ran behind pump 4, something ducked behind the counter inside. It was pouring now, sheets of putrid water washing red-brown water down the streets. From the car cabin, there were only shadows outside. The frame moaned, and the rain sounded as if there were thousands of evil spirits hammering the roof, all trying to tear in. 

|I don’t want to tell you how I feel.||

|There’s no point.||

Some many things scratched at the doors, like scalpels dragging at the paint. The overhead lights had gone out what seemed like years ago, and it was completely dark out now, like it had been dark the entire time they had been there. 

|| Sure there is.|

Something foul-smelling dripped from the ceiling, right onto the passenger’s seat, soaking the cushion.

|How’s that?||

A stream now, of bile-black chunky fluid, some now-invisible crack spewing this humor into the car, soaking the cushions. It was up to their ankles. Something tall stood in front of the passenger’s door, a shadow darker than everything else, a darker ink than black, a silhouette of pain in the window.  

||I don’t talk this way.|

The driver’s side door swung open and that hellish rain stopped as though someone flipped a switch. Those streetlights returned and not a trace of red ran into the storm drains. The clerk sat outside, smoking. He glanced over, and saw someone walk out of the now-empty car, away.
