# Recovery

*Last updated: {{ page.date | date_to_string }}*

Good on you for clicking random buttons in the footer of my website.

One year ago, on this date, I went to my first Narcotics Anonymous meeting. I
was a nervous wreck, and I honestly don't remember much from it. I remember
crying and getting so high afterwards I threw up in the toilet, which I promise
I don't normally do.

I was a pretty mentally ill kid. I was first hospitalized for a suicide attempt
when I was 12 years old, when I intentionally overdosed on clonidine and
fluoxetine (in all honestly, only one of those probably put me in any mortal
danger). 

This would begin my long history in the psychiatric wheelhouse. I did my first
stint at a children and adolescent psychiatric program in New Jersey.
Here, I met the first group of kids I really felt like understood the torture my
brain was putting me through. I connected with the other kids there in a way
that was impossible back home.

This is not to say that the psych ward was pleasant. While this was one
of the nicer psych wards I've been to (relatively clean, no quiet rooms, etc.),
it was still an involuntary hold. I was glad to be out when I was, and perhaps a
bit disgruntled that I had to attent an intensive outpatient program afterwards. 

My parents were convinced that this was a temporary blip in my life---what
parents would not be? If only for their own sanity? 

Unfortunately, the staff at that first hospital and the IOP/PHP program would
see me grow up. Every year, I would find myself back in the same bleak state,
unable to cope with life. Some years I would fling myself into traffic; other
times, pills; still others, I would readmit back to the hospital from partial.
It was always a bit embarassing to see the same psychiatrists at the hospital
whenever I went back, although I would typically see different nurses every
visit. 

All this time, I was relatively straightedge. My parents rarely drank, and my
father took great care to not expose me to his smoking habits (I smoke
Newports, FWIW). Although my parents never had a serious discussion with me
regarding safely using drugs, my friends were not really the type to use. I
heard a lot *about* drugs in my time in and out of the hospital and partials and
such (God, the xandemic...) but I never really had access to whatever my peers
were always getting in trouble over.

This changed once I made it to university. When you're trying to kill yourself
every year, it's a bit hard to imagine being an adult, let alone the next year.
Despite this, I found myself enrolled at Tufts University, states away from
where I grew up, and far away from my parents (my relationship with whom had
gotten really rocky in high school). I was in a failing relationship, and had
been out of the hospital for over a year at that point. My medications felt
pointless, and I felt like I could never feel okay (not that I ever had). 

At this point, I'm not particularly sure what my first drug was, but if I had to
guess, it was probably alcohol. Whenever I had some liquor in me, I felt free. I
felt like there were chains around me that were lifted for the first time in my
life. I felt, for lack of a better word, happy. I thought it was happiness,
anyhow---I had no frame of reference for "happiness". I wanted more. 

I will spare you all the gory details (trust me, there are many). 

My using steadily increased, and it quickly became a regular part of my routine.
If I needed to study, I crushed up a bunch of Adderall. If I wanted to relax, I
crushed a few beers and shots. If I wanted to *really* relax, I hit my weed pen
and maybe boosted some cough syrup from CVS for some DXM. 

Eventually, the drugs stopped becoming sufficient to numb my mental anguish. On
one Sunday last year, when I felt particularly terrible about my life, I decided
to get belligerently drunk (and probably high, too, but they don't screen for
DXM at the ER, so who knows). Unfortunately, this did not make me feel any
better, and so I decided that the best course of action was to take every pill
on my desk at once and to take my own life. 

The same dear friend I told you about earlier, the one who dragged me to my
first NA meeting a month(-ish) later, peeled me off my floor that night and
drove me to the ER. I had made myself really quite ill, and was due to be
admitted to the hospital generally when my condition rapidly improved a day
later. I was discharged from the ER and admitted to the psych ward, this time in
sunny, sunny Massachussetts. 

When I was being discharged from the psych ward, the psychiatrist there asked me
if I wanted to be put on naltrexone. I very adamantly said no. She asked me if
it was because I wanted to enjoy being drunk and high, and I said "yes" almost
flippantly. She then asked me if I had ever heard of 12-step programs. It did
not occur to me then that she was probably lightly suggesting that I may have a
problem with addiction.

In fact, I was so deep in denial about my addiction that I was high probably
within 24 hours of discharge from the psych ward. In my head, drugs were the
only thing that allowed me to deal with my suicidality. 

If you're thinking that that sounds illogical, it is! This was the insanity of
my addiction. I was so beholden to drugs that I swallowed untruths such as these
as easily as the pills I loved. 

My addiction reached its fever pitch in the month or so after my discharge from
the psych ward. I had to take medical leave from school following my overdose,
and so I was left structureless and listless. I was in an IOP, yes, but I didn't
really take it all that serious. It was just something to check off, something I
was supposed to do. My real recovery, I thought, was in my room, in the dark,
alone, high.

One of the few concrete things I remember about that first meeting is that, for
the first time in my life, I saw a little light. Listening to all these fucked
up addicts around me, how they struggled, were struggling, and yet they were all
in the same room as me, some of them doing better than I had ever been, made me
realize, even for a tiny second, that there was something more to life than
this. That something different was possible.

I was not ready for this possiblity at the time, and I got high as a way to
hide. However, I would keep going to meetings. I would often go to meetings and
get high afterwards and feel kinda bad about it and go back anyways. This
ridiculous cycle would continue on for about three weeks, when, after I did coke
at my dear friends house behind her back, she gave me an ultimatum that would
give me my first moment of clarity: either get clean, or she would not be able
to drive me around to meetings anymore. She was kind but firm about it. 

I realized that I didn't want to be alone. I wanted to go to meetings. I was
afraid of being alone my whole life. I saw the two paths that lay in front of
me---accept that I needed to get clean, and bask in the love of others, or keep
using, and die alone, just like I was always afraid of.
